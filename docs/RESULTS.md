### Against the ">95%" targets

- Ranking anomalies above normal windows: AUROC >= 0.99 met on all three encoders (1.000, 1.000, 0.997).
- Average precision >= 0.95: met on DSP (0.995) and ctx (0.968); CNN 0.887.
- Catching anomalies at about a 1% false-alarm budget: window-level alarm recall 0.995 / 1.000 / 0.96 at realised false-alarm rates of 1.1 to 1.8%. The 1% target is exceeded slightly, more after maintenance; [INFERENCE] wear drift and the maintenance step break the exchangeability that conformal calibration assumes.
- Rare-event retention: DSP and ctx keep every anomaly event and every seizure in about 40 slots (FIFO and reservoir keep at most 2.2% of events with 256). CNN keeps all seizures and every fault type but only 26.4% of events.
- These are results on our own synthetic generator, whose anomalies are strong by design. They do not transfer to field data without validation on real labelled machinery data (`docs/IMPROVEMENT-ANALYSIS.md`, item 11).

### Budget sweep

uEngram reaches AUROC 1.0 at every budget. At B=64 it keeps 91.9% of events and 67% of seizures in about 25 slots, so the episodic budget (16 slots at B=64) binds; from B=128 up it keeps all of them in about 40 slots and the budget is not binding. FIFO and reservoir keep at most 4.7% of events even at B=512.

### CMAPSS FD001

- RUL prediction improved from degenerate to the best among the stores: RMSE 42.7 → **22.9** (DSP) and 42.7 → **21.4** (CNN), against naive 25.8 / 23.9 and reservoir 24.2 / 23.5 (non-overlapping CIs). With noise whitening the memory keeps 193 slots (v1: 3 to 5), so recall returns life-stage-specific states. Published FD001 results are 11.4 to 16.1 (`docs/research-cmapss.md`); a memory lookup is not a trained RUL regressor.
- End-of-life detection got worse: AUROC 0.749 → 0.592 (DSP), 0.745 → 0.583 (CNN), now below naive (0.697 / 0.832) and reservoir. `eol_retention` fell from 1 to 0.40 / 0.47 with the semantic group full. [INFERENCE] Degradation is slow, so late-life states persist long enough to be promoted to normal states, and a late-life window then looks familiar. This is the "novelty is not health" problem from the analysis: detection needs a distance from a healthy baseline (diagnostic AUROC 0.9999), which is not implemented.
- `uengram_frozen` is identical to `uengram` on every CMAPSS metric.

### Where uEngram still loses or is unresolved

- **CNN encoder retention and writes.** `event_recall` 0.264, write reduction 65.3%, 222 slots. The step detector fires about 155 times per CNN run versus about 6 for DSP/ctx and fills the semantic group with superseded slots (`uengram_nostep`: 71 slots, 68.4%). [INFERENCE] The CNN latent drifts with wear along directions where its noise is small, so after noise whitening the slow drift looks like a sequence of steps.
- **Writes on DSP:** 86.1%, below 90%. The context encoder is the only configuration above 90%.
- **Doc engine behaviour depends on the encoder space.** `reference_labelfree` keeps every event with 27 slots in noise-whitened CNN space but has AUROC 0.944 and AP 0.236. On DSP its AUROC is 0.318.
- **Retrieval of the right fault type** (`type_prec_at1`) is 0.987 (DSP), 0.902 (ctx), 0.806 (CNN), against naive 0.988 / 0.929 / 0.901.

### Caveats

- On CMAPSS the DSP encoder and the stream are deterministic, so seed only changes the reservoir; CIs for the other DSP rows are a single value, not evidence of precision.
- The noise-whitening step changed every encoder, so baseline rows also differ from `results/v1/`; the v1-vs-v2 tables isolate uEngram only.
- `reference_oracle` uses ground-truth labels and is not a deployable configuration.
- Engine deviations from `docs/PLAN.md` and every v2 change are described in `docs/EXPLAINED.md`.
