# Condition-monitoring features and physics-informed residuals

## 1. Order tracking / angular resampling

A fixed-Hz spectrum shifts whenever shaft speed changes: the same shaft-synchronous component at order \(q\) appears at \(f=q f_r\), where \(f_r=\mathrm{RPM}/60\). Given tachometer phase \(\theta(t)=\int_0^t 2\pi f_r(\tau)\,d\tau\), resample the vibration as \(x_\theta[m]=x(t(\theta_m))\), uniformly in angle, then FFT against angle. Its peaks are orders \(q=f/f_r\) (cycles/revolution), so shaft-synchronous harmonics remain in the same coordinates across speeds. Computed order tracking estimates/resamples phase digitally; it requires adequate tachometer/phase estimation and angular sampling to avoid interpolation and aliasing errors. This is a coordinate normalization, not a cure for load-dependent amplitude or non-synchronous components.

**For this stream:** compute order-domain band energies / harmonics in each vibration axis, alongside time-domain impulsiveness. This should reduce regime-induced novelty from RPM shifts; retain absolute-Hz/high-frequency bands for resonances and broadband impacts.

## 2. Impulsive fault indicators

For demeaned samples \(x_i\), RMS \(x_{rms}=\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_i x_i^2}\), peak \(x_{pk}=\max_i|x_i|\). Crest factor \(CF=x_{pk}/x_{rms}\); excess kurtosis \(K=N^{-1}\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^4/s^4-3\), with \(s^2=N^{-1}\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2\). Both respond to sparse shocks, but crest factor can be dominated by a single outlier; kurtosis can be diluted by long windows or multiple background components. Spectral kurtosis (SK) measures impulsiveness separately across frequency bands; the **kurtogram** searches time-frequency resolution/bandwidth for an impulsive band, often selecting a band-pass filter before envelope analysis. Envelope analysis band-passes a resonant carrier, forms analytic signal \(a(t)=x_b(t)+j\mathcal H\{x_b(t)\}\), then examines \(|a(t)|\)'s spectrum for periodic impacts / bearing fault repetition rates.

For a knock made of three impulses plus a high-frequency tone, compare impact features (CF, excess kurtosis, SK/kurtogram peak, envelope burst energy) with order-normalized ordinary regime-vibration energy. Three repeated bursts may produce harmonic/event-rate structure in envelope spectrum; a pure tone alone is not impulsive and tends to have low kurtosis. A regime vibration change may raise RMS and order-band amplitude without a large impulsiveness increase. SK/envelope is strongest if impacts excite a structural resonance; no single indicator guarantees separation, so use temporal burst count / spacing and cross-channel evidence as well.

Sources: [Antoni & Randall (2006), journal record and abstract](https://hal.science/hal-01714436): SK is described as useful for incipient faults amid masking noise; kurtogram selects optimal band-pass filters, e.g. before envelope analysis. [Randall & Antoni (2011), “Rolling element bearing diagnostics—a tutorial,” MSSP](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymssp.2010.07.017); [publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0888327010002530) (publisher page returned HTTP 403; title and citation surfaced in search metadata).

## 3. Pump cavitation / pressure ripple; thermal residuals

For pressure signal \(p_i\), trend-remove / subtract the operating-condition mean, then track pressure ripple \(\sigma_p^2=N^{-1}\sum(p_i-\bar p)^2\), peak-to-peak, and high-frequency band power \(E_{HF}=\sum_{f\in B_{HF}}|P(f)|^2\); also consider broadband acoustic/vibration energy and pump blade-pass/order components. Cavitation bubble formation/collapse changes pressure-pulsation, vibration, acoustic/noise and motor-current signatures. Compare at matched flow, speed, and suction pressure: pump pressure variance is not a universal threshold independent of operating point. A reviewed experimental study reports cavitation classification from decomposed motor-current components using energy share, variance, kurtosis, skewness, and RMS; VMD binary result was 92.30% overall on its test apparatus, underscoring that performance is setup-specific, not a portable guarantee.

For thermal state use a heat-balance model where feasible: \(C\,dT/dt=Q_{in}(L,n)-h(L,n)(T-T_{amb})\). A one-step first-order approximation is \(\hat T_{t+1}=T_t+\Delta t\,[Q_{in}(L_t,n_t)-h_t(T_t-T_{amb,t})]/C\); simpler identified lag: \(\hat T_{t+1}=a(L_t,n_t)T_t+[1-a(L_t,n_t)]T_{eq}(L_t,n_t)\), \(a=e^{-\Delta t/\tau}\). Use residual \(r_T=T_{meas}-\hat T\), with uncertainty scaled by condition and model error; persistent signed / cumulative residuals can indicate cooling, sensor, or load-model faults. Do not flag a normal transient merely because \(T\) lags an abrupt load change—the state equation predicts the lag.

Sources: [Liang et al. (2023), pump cavitation study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/energy-research/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2023.1204300/full): pressure pulsation, vibration, noise, and current are established signal families; their VMD-based binary classification reports 92.30% overall accuracy. [Yoo et al. ## 4. Normalise by operating condition

Instead of one global feature mean/covariance, model expected feature \(f\) conditional on operating covariates \(u=(n,L,\text{load},\text{ambient},\text{regime})\). For each feature, fit \(\hat\mu_j(u)\) and conditional scale \(\hat\sigma_j(u)\) on trusted healthy data, then \(z_j=[f_j-\hat\mu_j(u)]/\max(\hat\sigma_j(u),\epsilon)\). Flexible choices include regime-bin robust median/MAD, smooth regression / GAM, or normal-behaviour regression. For multivariate residuals \(e=f-\hat f(u)\), score with \(e^T\Sigma(u)^{-1}e\); estimate condition-specific covariance only with enough data and regularization. Keep slow wear / maintenance changes as state drift or changepoints rather than normalizing them away. Unknown / sparsely observed regimes should be uncertainty-aware, not extrapolated with false confidence.

Wind-turbine SCADA normal-behaviour modelling predicts expected outputs from operating variables and monitors prediction deviations; Schlechtingen & Santos describe models on 10-minute averaged SCADA data and application examples. This is an analogy for speed/load-conditioned residuals, not direct validation on engines.

## 5. Learned vibration encoders: benefits and traps

An autoencoder trains \(\min_\theta \|x-g_\theta(f_\theta(x))\|^2\) on nominal data, then uses reconstruction error. It may learn an identity-like map and reconstruct faults well; conversely, benign regime or phase shifts can inflate error. Window-level MSE can hide short impact bursts, while sensor scaling controls the loss. Validate on held-out operating conditions and fault types; compare reconstruction residual, latent distance / density, and physically meaningful features. A latent distance detects atypical encoded states even when decoded well, but can itself group distinct states or discard fault information. Use channel/scale-aware losses and augmentations that preserve fault semantics.

Contrastive/self-supervised alternatives learn representations from unlabeled data. TS2Vec uses temporal + instance contrast with hierarchical multi-scale pooling and contextual consistency; in its paper it improved average classification accuracy by 2.4% on 125 UCR and 3.0% on 29 UEA datasets over the best compared unsupervised representation, and reported anomaly-detection F1 gains on Yahoo/KPI benchmarks. TF-C aligns temporal and frequency-domain representations via contrastive pretraining; on eight datasets (including mechanical fault detection) authors report average +15.4% F1 in one-to-one transfer and +8.4% precision in one-to-many transfer versus baselines. These are published benchmark results, not expected gains on this engine stream. For uEngram, train/test splits must be machine- and time-separated; learn embeddings that preserve impact/band/order information rather than invariance to it.

Sources: [TS2Vec (AAAI 2022), full primary paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/20881/20640): metrics, design and benchmarks above. [TF-C (NeurIPS 2022), arXiv primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08496): time-frequency consistency and reported transfer benefits.

## 6. Temporal context: make expected transitions predictable

A single-window novelty score ignores the path into a state. Add first differences \(\Delta z_t=z_t-z_{t-1}\), optionally condition on \(u_t\), or score an autoregressive prediction residual \(e_t=z_t-\hat z_t\), where \(\hat z_t=A z_{t-1}+B u_t\) (or nonlinear model). More generally, model \(p(z_t\mid z_{t-k:t-1},u_{t-k:t})\) and use negative log-likelihood / whitened residual as anomaly score. Stack adjacent embeddings \([z_{t-k+1},…,z_t]\), or explicitly model a regime transition and expected thermal lag. This lets a predictable ramp after a regime switch be normal while an unexpected departure from the predicted ramp is anomalous. Deltas alone can amplify noise; use robust filtering / calibrated residual covariance and preserve a separate absolute-state score so persistent offset faults are not erased.

These are direct model-design recommendations, not claims of a single published optimum; references above support normal-behaviour residuals, and TS2Vec supports contextual multi-scale representations.
